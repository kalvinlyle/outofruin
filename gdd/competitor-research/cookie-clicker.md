# Cookie Clicker — the genre-defining browser idle game, its humour and the Steam relaunch

## Snapshot

- **Studio:** Julien "Orteil" Thiennot (solo creator). Original browser release published by DashNet. Steam and console releases published by Playsaurus. Source: [Wikipedia: Cookie Clicker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_Clicker).
- **Platforms:** Web browser (August 8, 2013), Android (mobile beta August 8, 2019; listed release October 5, 2020), Windows and macOS via Steam (September 1, 2021), PlayStation 4/5, Xbox One/Series X/S and Nintendo Switch (May 22, 2025). Nintendo Switch 2 is listed as planned. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_Clicker).
- **Release and status:** Active across browser, mobile, Steam and consoles over more than a decade. Orteil's Patreon was set to close January 24, 2026, per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_Clicker).
- **Monetisation:** Browser version is free. The Steam version was paid; a tweet cited on Wikipedia mentions a $4.99 price. Patreon support was the creator's side channel. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_Clicker).
- **Audience scale (non-audited):** The Steam release reached over 60,000 concurrent players shortly after launch. Wikipedia estimates about 4 million daily active users across all platforms. Treat both as soft, non-audited figures. Achievement-earn counts on Exophase are a secondary signal only.
- **Review sentiment:** Strong on Steam at launch. Early reviews were reported at 98% "overwhelmingly positive" from about 5,000 reviews, per [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/cookie-clicker-has-quickly-become-one-of-the-most-popular-games-on-steam-3038588). Critics are split on substance. IGN called it the "greatest Idle Game," while Destructoid described an "illusion of progress, without any substantial advancement." Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_Clicker).
- **Player promise:** Click a cookie, build a bakery empire, and ascend into a silly, self-referential cosmic joke that keeps escalating.

## Key innovations / what it does well

- **Genre-defining core loop.** The original game made idle clicking into a whole genre. It reached 50,000 players within hours of a 4chan link after its August 2013 release. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_Clicker).
- **Humour as a retention system.** Dark jokes sit in upgrade names and achievements, such as "Global Warming" for owning 100 factories. A news ticker reads "New cookie-based religion sweeps the nation." The game parodies FarmVille and runs cosmic-horror and apocalypse threads, such as the "Grandmapocalypse." Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_Clicker).
- **Achievements that change the world.** The Steam release has 637 achievements. Unlocking enough of them adds new milk colours, and kitten upgrades appear with each new milk. Achievements reward exploration rather than just counting. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_Clicker); [Exophase](https://www.exophase.com/game/cookie-clicker-steam/prices/) for the count.
- **Heavenly Chips prestige.** Ascending resets progress and grants Heavenly Chips and prestige levels. Each prestige level adds +1% production, and chips buy permanent upgrades. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_Clicker).
- **Steam relaunch with cloud saves and achievements.** The 2021 Steam release brought achievements and cloud saves to a game that had been free in the browser for eight years, and it held a top spot on the platform. Source: [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/cookie-clicker-is-now-on-steam); [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/cookie-clicker-has-quickly-become-one-of-the-most-popular-games-on-steam-3038588).

## Player frictions & complaints

- **Illusion of progress.** Destructoid's criticism is that the cookie focus creates progress without substantive advancement. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_Clicker).
- **Dismissal as too simple.** Some critics call it "super dumb" for its simple mechanics. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_Clicker).
- **Hidden achievements.** Some "shadow achievements" are judged too unfair or difficult to complete, so players see them as an obscure checklist. Source: search-result summary of [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_Clicker) and related coverage; not independently checked against Steam data.
- **Background-running concerns.** Fans have noted the energy cost of computers left running and reduced productivity at work. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_Clicker).
- **Paid Steam launch versus free browser.** The browser version stayed free while Steam was paid, which some players noticed as a split between free and paid tiers. Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_Clicker).

## Lessons for Out of Ruin

- **Let humour carry the milestone text.** Cookie Clicker's dark jokes in upgrade names and achievements make an otherwise repetitive loop feel authored. Out of Ruin's light fantasy humour should sit in henchman names, chapter titles and achievement text, not just in the story.
- **Make achievements change the world.** Cookie Clicker's milk colours and kittens give each achievement a visible effect. Out of Ruin's milestones should unlock a visible change on the dungeon map or in the henchman roster.
- **Keep the browser version free and the premium tier additive.** Cookie Clicker's browser game stayed free after the paid Steam release, and its reach came from that free base. Out of Ruin's web-first, free-to-play entry should stay complete without gold.
- **Answer "illusion of progress" with a visible strategic choice.** Cookie Clicker's Heavenly Chips and AdVenture Capitalist's Angel Investors both give the reset a meaningful decision. Out of Ruin's rank-up reset should present clear trade-offs, not just a bigger number.
- **Relaunch with the platform's own expectations.** The Steam release added achievements and cloud saves on top of a browser game. Out of Ruin's web relaunch should treat cloud-style saves and clear achievements as baseline expectations for its audience.
