# Out of Ruin — Competitor Analysis

Out of Ruin sits at an unusual intersection: a **fantasy RPG roster** (heroes, henchmen, gear cards) on top of a **cascading idle economy**, delivered **install-free on the mobile web**.

The mobile idle market splits into games with deep rosters that sell power, and games players call fair that have shallow rosters.
This analysis maps that split and where Out of Ruin can fit.

Positions and chronology summaries here are editorial; the sourced depth is in the per-game files.

## Closest neighbours

- **[AdVenture Communist](./competitor-research/adventure-communist.md)** — the same backbone: quest-driven ranks on a cascading economy, with collectible modifiers and a strong comic voice.
- **[Tap Titans 2](./competitor-research/tap-titans-2.md)** — the same tap-to-fight fantasy with heroes; the commercial bar for the genre.
- **[Idle Miner Tycoon](./competitor-research/idle-miner-tycoon.md)** — the economy twin: managers running a production chain across regions, like henchmen across dungeons.
- **[Idle Slayer](./competitor-research/idle-slayer.md)** — the closest posture: a small team, fantasy theme, active play and fair monetisation.
- **[AFK Arena](./competitor-research/afk-arena.md)** — the ceiling for heroes and gear, and the clearest example of idle income tied to chapter progress.

## How Out of Ruin differs

**The landscape-wide pattern:** the games with deep rosters sell power, and the games players call fair have shallow rosters.
AFK Arena, Idle Heroes and Tap Titans 2 make heroes and gear the heart of the game and monetise them through gacha, VIP tiers or clan gear; their communities complain about walls and pay-to-win.
Egg, Inc., Idle Slayer and Cookie Clicker earn goodwill with fair stores, but their collections are thin.
AdVenture Communist sits between and loses trust exactly where its events and random researchers meet spending.
The plot in [competitor-axis.html](./competitor-axis.html) shows the top-right, deep and fair, empty.

Out of Ruin's wedges work as a system; removing any one weakens the rest:

1. **A real roster, earned by playing.** Heroes, henchmen and gear cards give the build depth of the collectors, with odds published, a pity counter on packs, and duplicates convertible into the card a player is missing. Without the roster this is another shallow fair idler; without the fairness it is another collector.
2. **Honest progression.** Chapters advance through quests and loot, never through an event or a random drop. A future event layer stays winnable for free players who play actively. This answers the two most-upvoted quitting complaints in the AdVenture Communist community.
3. **A cascade players can read.** Higher-level henchmen produce lower-level henchmen, the same snowball that makes AdVenture Communist and Idle Miner Tycoon satisfying, shown on one screen with its choke point visible.
4. **Tap combat with hero powers.** The production idlers have no fight; Out of Ruin has monsters, crits and hero powers to time, which gives the active moments Idle Slayer is loved for.
5. **Zero-install play.** A link opens the game in a portrait browser; every competitor in the set is store-first.
6. **Comic fantasy voice.** Theme and humour are a tenth of AdVenture Communist's community posts; Out of Ruin's henchmen and dungeons carry the same kind of voice.

**The rocks the closest analogues sank on:**

- **Content drought and late walls.** Tap Titans 2 and AdVenture Communist players describe the late game as waiting. Out of Ruin has 11 chapters and 10 unnamed henchmen.
- **Event fairness.** AdVenture Communist's revenue and community peaked with events, and its loudest quitting threads came from events tuned for spenders.
- **Lost progress.** Browser saves can be cleared; save export, persistent storage or accounts are needed before real-money sales scale.
- **Too few reasons to return.** Without events, leaderboards or co-op, nothing brings a lapsed player back; Egg, Inc.'s contracts show a social layer can work without full accounts.
