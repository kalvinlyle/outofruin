# Changelog

## 2026-11-25

[feature] Full game audio: Scenario SFX, adaptive music and harmonic cascade engine | 2026-11-25 | 25m — Added 49 hand-crafted fantasy SFX cues and 4 instrumental music beds (Homeland, Ruins, Mystic Events, Siege Events) generated via Scenario (`model_elevenlabs-sound-effects-v2` + `model_elevenlabs-music`) and baked through `tools/audio/bake.mjs` to lean AAC-LC `.m4a` files (~3.97 MB total) with leading-silence stripping, natural-decay trimming, loudness normalisation, loop-seam crossfades and `.meta` sidecars stripped from `game/dist/`. Built `port/src/systems/audio.ts` with a dedicated SFX true-peak limiter, music sidechain ducking, menu low-pass filtering, max-2-buffer RAM eviction, gapless scheduled looping, off-screen and catch-up burst suppression, and a D-major pentatonic harmonic cascade engine wired across taps, combat, crits, hiring, chest reveals, card upgrades, quests, rank-ups, shop, time warps, hero powers and independent Sound/Music toggles and volume bars at the top of Settings.

## 2026-10-26

[feature] Android app shell, analytics and Play Billing | 2026-10-26 | 3h — The game now builds as an Android app (native/, Capacitor 8): fullscreen, portrait, the game's own background colour, signed release builds with the commit count as versionCode and the web bundle version as versionName, and a GitHub Actions workflow that tests, builds and uploads the AAB to the Play internal track. PostHog (new EU project) and Snowplow are fed from the shipped game's own Google Analytics calls through one track(), with the device as the player on native, and AppsFlyer attribution rides beside them; keys reach each build through a staged env.js, and production builds refuse to ship without them. Gold packs sell through Google Play Billing in the app, each purchase checked with Google before its gold is granted. Privacy, terms and data deletion pages, the Play listing kit and the human console checklist are in place, and the game is registered in FOUNDRY.

[feature] Live updates for the native app | 2026-10-26 | 2h — The game's Vercel deployment now serves its own over-the-air updates to the native app through a self-hosted @capgo/capacitor-updater endpoint (game/api/updates.js), ported from match-merge. Every deploy stages game/dist and packs it as a zip plus per-file blobs named by sha256, so an installed player downloads only what changed (0.61 MB of 79 MB for a code change). The last three versions stay hosted for rollback. game/ota-version.json picks what the public and an opt-in beta get, and the build refuses a pointer at an unhosted version. The app checks on launch and on every return, rolls back a bundle that never draws a frame, has a Beta updates row in settings, and checkpoints the save so leaving the beta cannot lose it. The Capacitor shell config is in native/; see docs/ota-updates.md.

[refactor] The game moves from Defold to an HTML/WebGL port | 2026-10-26 | 16h — The game now runs on a TypeScript and WebGL rebuild of the Defold 1.2.167 runtime instead of the Defold engine, so GUI scenes are no longer capped at 1024 nodes (the cap had hidden the header on 20:9 phones in late chapters). Every Lua script was translated line by line onto a Lua 5.1 compatibility layer checked against a real Lua binary, and a Playwright harness replays 39 scenarios at three screen sizes on both builds: all 117 runs match frame for frame (≥99% pixels), with identical game state and byte-identical saves, and existing saves load unchanged. Late game runs about 35% faster than Defold under 4x CPU throttling. Game code now lives in port/src/game; the last Defold version is tagged defold-final.

[feature] Three threat events replace the bubblegum ones | 2026-10-26 | 2h — The Salt Tide, The Clockwork Carnival and The Long Frost were rejected as cosy company-event fantasy and never went live. In their place, three enemy events where a town in trouble sends for adventurers: What Sleeps in Titans Well (Warden Holt, the drowned and the Abyssal Maw, hero Nell Hookline), The Midnight Carnival (Constable Vane, the Mirror Twin, the Strongman and the Ringmaster, hero Fizzwick Cogsworth) and The Longest Night (Elder Osric, midwinter raiders and the Winter King, hero Barnaby Hearth). Each has three locations of enemies and bosses painted in ARTSTYLE's moody boss style, its own tinted XP star, chest, book, map and gear, and a part 1 that tells the story from the call for help to the final fight, on the Blackmaw Siege's quest structure. The rotation runs Wererabbit, Blackmaw, then the three. tools/remove_atlas_frames.py frees dead frames from a full atlas; the old events' frames and art are gone.

[feature] Three more live events and a "Something stirs" pin | 2026-10-26 | 1h — The weekly rotation grows from one event to four: Night of the Wererabbit, then The Salt Tide (Harbourmaster Brine, Pearls, clam chest, Nell Hookline), The Clockwork Carnival (Ringmaster Spindle, Golden Tickets, prize box, Fizzwick Cogsworth) and The Long Frost (Gaffer Tallow, Embers, hearth-stone chest, Barnaby Hearth), on maps 3-5. Each has the Wererabbit's numbers level for level with its own three locations, 17 drops, 9 henchmen, 27 gear cards, map painting, chapter book, chest, quest-giver and lines; part 1 is the Wererabbit's quests rethemed and parts 2-10 are generated. While an event runs, the next one's place on the world map shows a purple "Something stirs" pin counting down to its start, with a teaser on tap; event places are chosen so a phone frames the live pin and that one together, and a saved map view is re-framed when the pins change. The schedule moves into actions.lua, a finished part counts down to the event's next run, the event bar shrinks a long name to fit, and a popup raised while the game boots picks up its strings once they load. Event content lives in tools/event_content.py; the map, items_drops, items_gear, lootbox, npc_portraits and reward_icons atlases grew to fit.

## 2026-10-25

[ui] Level-ups ask before spending XP, and the XP flies into the card | 2026-10-25 | 30m — Tapping Level Up on a henchman, gear or hero card spent XP (or an event card's Moon Shards) at once. The upgrade card screen now opens as a confirmation: the card at its current level, "Upgrade to level N", the exact cost with its currency icon, and Upgrade / Cancel. A chip at the top stands in for the header's XP slot with the player's balance of that currency; on Upgrade the spend streams out of the chip in a fan of icons (more for bigger costs) into the card while the chip counts down, the card thumps as each lands, and the new level swaps in after the last. Cancel goes back with nothing spent.

[balance] Event hires cost Followers and Moon Shards become the event's XP | 2026-10-25 | 1h — Event hires cost a Moon Shard while the main game charged a Follower, and event cards levelled up with the main game's XP. Event hires now cost one Follower from the main pool, the same as a main-game hire, and Moon Shards work as the event's XP: event henchmen and gear level up with them, and event items' rank-ups pay them where main-game items pay XP. While the player is on the event map, the header's XP slot shows the shards. Closing a main-game chapter leaves event items' shard bonuses alone, and a new run clears them. Hazel's intro and lines, the moon chest text and gdd/live-events.md say so.

[ui] World map frames the event, zooms, and remembers its view | 2026-10-25 | 2h — The world map opened at full size on one corner, with pin titles running out of their boxes, only the "!" opening a map, and the back button floating in from the corner on wider screens. It now opens framed on the pins (Your Lands and the live event's; on a narrow phone the event's is the one kept in full), never zooms out past the map's edges, drags, zooms with a pinch or the mouse wheel, and keeps its zoom and position in the save (Stats.WorldMapView). Pins keep their size on screen at any zoom and draw over the other locations, each pin's box grows to its text, the home pin drops under its castle when it would cover the event's, and a tap on a pin's label opens its map. The back button override places it against the real top-left corner on every screen. patch_game.py adds a multi-touch trigger to the input binding for the pinch.

[feature] Events become a 100-chapter story with collectible henchmen, gear and moon chests | 2026-10-25 | 2h — Each event is now 100 chapters from its quest-giver, played a part of ten per run; finishing a part ends the event for the player until it comes round, and a run that ends mid-part resumes at the chapter reached. Part 1 is Hazel's written tiers; parts 2–10 come from a seeded generator (collect, tap a level, hire on a level, send a hero, open the way on) with a pool of Hazel's lines per drop and quest kind, so no chapter repeats. Event henchmen and gear are kept from run to run, and each part's goals grow 1.5x while level health is set from the crew's strength when a run starts, so a part still takes about the week. Every chapter pays a normal chest and a new moon chest holding only event cards and Moon Shards; part 1 ends with the Human Cleric, later parts with 10–90 of his cards. Twenty-seven event gear cards (three per event level) are in the items_gear atlas, grown to 2048x4096, and the moon chest is in the lootbox atlas. The cards screen gets Main and Events tabs with a tab per event; event heroes show locked in Main and say which event earns them.

[feature] Twenty new heroes, art for all of them, and the five shipped heroes repainted | 2026-10-25 | 6h — The five shipped heroes are repainted in the stylised card style, coloured by their virtues (first virtue lights the figure, second is the background, third a small highlight; palette in ARTSTYLE.md), and the marketing site's roster cards are rebuilt from them. Twenty new heroes from gdd/heroes-roster.md are in the game, locked until their unlocks are designed: ten field heroes with new powers (Hex, Breach, Gone Fishing, Pounce, Clockwork Turret, Transmute, Muster, Echo, Hopscotch, Ballad of Deeds) and ten patrons whose passive bonuses cover offline income, Farm speed, hire costs, loot crits, XP, sell prices, card packs, time warps, quest slots and other heroes' levels. Every power is drawn where it lands: runes, rams, wolves, turrets, hops and catches fly from the hero's card to the bars and loot icons with counted numbers, and each patron appears in person on the screen their bonus touches. Nell's catches add four junk items to sell. The hero_portraits, main and power_vfx atlas pages are grown to fit the new art.
